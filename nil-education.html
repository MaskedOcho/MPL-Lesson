Following the Rules

To protect your eligibility and your future:

  • Talk to your school's compliance office before signing anything
  • Follow NCAA, conference, and college-specific policies
  • Disclose your NIL deals as required
  • Ask questions — staying informed protects your eligibility
  • Never use your school's marks, logos, or facilities in promotions without written permission
  • Don't accept compensation tied to athletic performance or enrollment
  • Avoid deals in prohibited categories — alcohol, gambling, tobacco, adult content, and similar industries
NIL Tax Basics

Always consult a tax professional for guidance specific to you.

  • 1099-NEC reports nonemployee compensation from brand deals
  • 1099-K reports payments made through third-party platforms like PayPal or Venmo
  • W-9 gives payers your tax information before they pay you
  • Set aside 25–35% of every NIL payment for taxes
  • Track all income and expenses year-round, not just at tax time
  • Consider quarterly estimated tax payments to avoid surprises
  • NIL athletes are typically treated as independent contractors, not employees
  • Ordinary and necessary business expenses may be deductible

Protect Yourself

What most athletes don't know.

Agent Fees

Agents commonly take 10–20% of your earnings — know the cost before you sign.

Contract Risks

Watch for exclusivity clauses, automatic renewals, ownership language, and long-term rights that outlast the deal.

Protect Your Identity

Never give up ownership of your name, image, likeness, or future content — no matter how good the offer looks.

NIL Collectives

Most collectives are donor-funded, not guaranteed income — funding can change year to year.

Turn NIL into long-term wealth.

  • Build your personal brand and grow your audience
  • Focus on long-term partnerships over one-off deals
  • Create your own products for ownership and higher margins
  • Build an email list to reach your audience directly

  • Agents are optional; collectives are not the same as NIL deals
