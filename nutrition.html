The Building Blocks

Macronutrients: build, fuel, support.

Macronutrients are the nutrients your body needs in large amounts to fuel performance, support growth, and maintain daily function. There's no single "perfect" nutrient — it's the balance that creates results.

Protein

Build. Repair. Recover.
  • Muscle repair and growth
  • Supports immune & hormone function
  • Preserves lean muscle during fat loss
  • Provides lasting satiety
Best sources: chicken, turkey, lean beef, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, lentils, protein powder.

Carbohydrates

Fuel. Perform. Endure.
  • Primary fuel for high-intensity activity
  • Stored in muscles and liver
  • Improves endurance, delays fatigue
  • Supports brain function
Best sources: oats, rice, whole grain pasta, quinoa, whole grain bread, beans, lentils, fruit.

Fats

Support. Protect. Balance.
  • Supports hormone production
  • Aids absorption of vitamins A, D, E, K
  • Provides long-lasting energy
  • Supports brain and heart health
  • Reduces inflammation
Best sources: avocados, nuts, seeds, olive oil, nut butters, fatty fish, eggs.

Hydration & Electrolytes

The foundation of performance.

Water is essential for every function in your body. When you train, sweat, and go about your day, you lose fluid and electrolytes — both must be replaced to perform and recover at your best.

The Key Electrolytes
  • Sodium — maintains fluid balance and supports nerve function.
  • Potassium — supports muscle function and heart rhythm.
  • Magnesium — aids muscle relaxation and reduces cramping.
  • Calcium — important for muscle contraction and bone health.
  • Chloride — helps maintain fluid balance and digestion.
Hydration Strategy
  • BeforeDrink 16–24 oz of water 2–3 hours before activity, then another 8–12 oz 10–20 minutes before you start.
  • DuringDrink 7–10 oz every 10–20 minutes and include electrolytes for longer sessions.
  • AfterRefuel within 2 hours, drinking 16–24 oz of fluid for every pound of body weight lost.
  • How Much Will You SweatWeigh yourself before and after exercise — for every pound lost, replace it with roughly 16–32 oz of fluid.
  • Electrolyte ReplenishmentReplace electrolytes based on your sweat loss, intensity, duration, and environment — through sports drinks, electrolyte tablets, coconut water, or whole foods.

What Dehydration Can Cause

  • Decreased strength & endurance
  • Increased fatigue
  • Slower reaction time
  • Higher perceived effort
  • Muscle cramps
  • Headaches & dizziness
  • Impaired focus & decision-making
  • Increased heat-illness risk

Tip: Urine color is a simple, reliable indicator of hydration — pale yellow means you're well hydrated, dark yellow means it's time to drink up.

Daily Habits

Replenish every day.

Fluid and electrolyte loss isn't just a workout problem — heat, travel, school days, and everyday activity all add up. Building daily habits keeps you topped off before you ever step on court.

Everyday Sources of Loss
  • Heat & humidity
  • Air travel & car rides
  • Long school or study days
  • Illness & poor sleep
How to Replenish Daily
  • Drink water consistently, not just around workouts
  • Add electrolytes on hot or high-sweat days
  • Eat water-rich foods — fruit, vegetables, soup
  • Keep a water bottle with you at all times
Daily Habits for Balance
  • Drink a glass of water first thing in the morning
  • Sip fluids steadily through the day, not all at once
  • Check in with yourself before and after activity
  • Prioritize sleep — it affects fluid balance too
Signs You Need More
  • Fatigue
  • Headache
  • Dark urine
  • Muscle cramps
  • Dizziness
Timing Matters

Pre & post-workout nutrition.

What you eat before and after you train shapes how well you perform and how fast you bounce back.

Before You Train

Carbs. Moderate Protein. Light Fat.
  • Carb-focused to top off energy stores
  • Moderate protein to support muscle
  • Low-to-moderate fat so digestion stays easy
  • Hydrate well in the hours beforehand
Try: oatmeal with banana, or toast with peanut butter.

After You Train

Replenish. Repair. Rehydrate.
  • Replenish glycogen with quality carbs
  • Restore protein to repair muscle tissue
  • Replace electrolytes lost in sweat
  • Aim to eat within 30–90 minutes of finishing
Try: grilled chicken with rice and vegetables, or a protein smoothie with fruit.

The Anabolic Window: The 30–90 minutes after training is when your muscles are primed to absorb nutrients and start repairing. Eating a balanced meal or snack in that window helps you recover faster and get more out of every session.
