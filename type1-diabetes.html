Understanding Type 1 Diabetes

Type 1 Diabetes is an autoimmune condition in which the body stops producing insulin. Insulin is required for glucose to enter cells and be used for energy. Exercise, nutrition, stress, illness, and other factors can cause blood sugar levels to rise or fall.

With proper education and preparation, athletes with Type 1 Diabetes can perform at the highest levels while staying safe.

How Exercise Affects Blood Sugar
  • Aerobic ExerciseActivities like running, cycling, and swimming usually lower blood sugar levels.
  • High Intensity ExerciseShort bursts of intense activity can cause blood sugar to rise temporarily.
  • Strength TrainingWeight training can lower or raise blood sugar depending on intensity and duration.
  • Competition StressAdrenaline and stress hormones can increase blood sugar levels.
Essential Safety Habits
  • Carry fast-acting carbohydrates at all times
  • Wear medical identification
  • Monitor glucose before, during, and after activity
  • Have an emergency plan — know what to do and who to contact
  • Communicate with coaches about your needs
  • Know the early warning signs of hypoglycemia
